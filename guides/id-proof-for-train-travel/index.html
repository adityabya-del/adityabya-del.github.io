Valid ID proof for train travel on an IRCTC e-ticket

You do not type any ID details when you book an e-ticket, but one passenger on the ticket must show an original ID during the journey. This guide lists what IRCTC accepts.

Last checked: 19 November 2026. Railway rules change, so confirm on IRCTC before you book or travel.

Do you need an ID to book?

No. IRCTC says the user does not have to enter the photo identity card details of any passenger while booking an e-ticket. Instead, one of the passengers on the ticket has to carry one of the listed identity cards in original and show it during the journey.

The IDs IRCTC accepts

IRCTC's Terms and Conditions list these as authorised IDs for travel on an e-ticket:

  • Passport
  • Voter photo identity card issued by the Election Commission of India
  • Driving licence issued by an RTO
  • PAN card issued by the Income Tax Department
  • Photo identity card with a serial number issued by the Central or State Government
  • Student identity card with photograph issued by a recognised school or college
  • Nationalised bank passbook with photograph
  • Credit card issued by a bank with a laminated photograph
  • Printed Aadhaar card or downloaded Aadhaar (e-Aadhaar)
  • m-Aadhaar, shown in the m-Aadhaar app on the passenger's phone after entering the password
  • Photo identity card with a serial number issued by a Central or State public sector undertaking, a District Administration, a municipal body or a Panchayat Administration
  • Aadhaar or driving licence shown from the "Issued Documents" section of the passenger's DigiLocker account

DigiLocker: issued, not uploaded

IRCTC accepts an Aadhaar or driving licence shown from the Issued Documents section after logging into DigiLocker. A document the user uploaded themselves, in the Uploaded Documents section, is not accepted as proof of identity.

One ID covers the whole ticket

The ID of any one passenger booked on the ticket is enough. A Railway Board circular of 16 November 2021 repeats the rule: in a reserved class, one passenger on the ticket has to produce one of the prescribed IDs in original, failing which all the passengers on that ticket are treated as travelling without a ticket.

Concession and quota passengers need proof too

The same circular says anyone travelling on a concession, or on a quota kept for a particular group, must carry valid proof that they qualify. Its examples are the concession certificate for Divyangjan passengers, a doctor's certificate for pregnant women booked on the quota kept for them, and age proof for senior citizens booked on the senior citizen quota.

Foreign passengers

For passengers who are not Indian, IRCTC says their own country's passport is the only accepted ID. On a Foreign Tourist quota ticket, the original passport and a valid visa must be shown; IRCTC says a passenger who cannot show them is treated as travelling without a ticket and charged a penalty of three times the fare. See our Foreign Tourist quota guide.

ID but no ticket on your phone?

If you cannot show the e-ticket (for example, the phone is lost or its battery is dead) but you do have the original ID, IRCTC says a penalty under Railway rules is charged and the ticket checking staff issue an Excess Fare Ticket. More in our guide on showing an e-ticket on your phone.

In short

  • No ID is entered at booking, but one passenger on the ticket must show an original ID on the train.
  • DigiLocker counts only from "Issued Documents".
  • Without any accepted ID, everyone on the ticket is treated as ticketless.
